## What This Does
TakeMeter classifies public running-forum posts into `analysis`, `hot_take`, or `reaction`. The community is a public running discussion forum represented by the provided forum-style corpus. `analysis` uses a load-bearing checkable fact, `hot_take` makes a general unsupported claim, and `reaction` reports an immediate personal response. The goal is a consistent triage signal, not a judgment of whether an opinion is true.

## Label Taxonomy

<!-- Each label: a one-sentence definition and two real examples from your
     reading. Then your decision rule for the hardest boundary.

     The decision rule is worth a point on its own and it's the thing most
     people leave out. Every taxonomy has a hardest boundary. Name yours. -->

### `analysis`

**Definition:** Makes an argument backed by a specific, checkable fact that does work in the argument.

**Example 1:**
> Compared my 5k splits from January and June; the second half improved by 18 seconds while weekly mileage stayed within two miles.

**Example 2:**
> The route was measured 120 metres short, which explains why this year's personal bests look unusually fast.

### `hot_take`

**Definition:** Makes a confident general claim without offering support for it.

**Example 1:**
> The marathon is overrated; the half is the only distance that matters.

**Example 2:**
> Strength training is a distraction for distance runners.

### `reaction`

**Definition:** Reports an immediate personal response to something that just happened.

**Example 1:**
> Watching that finish live left me genuinely shaking.

**Example 2:**
> I signed up for my first marathon and feel excited and terrified.

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** `analysis` vs `hot_take`

**The decision rule I used every time:** If a specific fact is load-bearing and the argument depends on it, choose `analysis`; if the fact is decorative or no support is offered, choose `hot_take`. If the post is mainly an immediate personal feeling, choose `reaction` even when it is emphatic.
<!-- e.g. "If the post names a specific checkable fact, it's `analysis`, even
     if the tone is heated." -->

## The Training Run

<!-- Your starting model, your settings, and anything you changed and why. -->

**Base model:** `distilbert-base-uncased`

**Settings:** 3 epochs, learning rate `2e-5`, batch size 16, maximum length 128, seeds 42, 7, and 2024.

**Anything I changed from the defaults, and why:** I kept the starter defaults so the first result is reproducible and comparable across seeds.

**Split sizes:** 140 train / 30 validation / 30 test, stratified. The exact per-label counts are recorded in the generated results and held-out split.

**Completed run:** `results.json` records seed 42 on Apple Silicon MPS. Test accuracy was `0.967` and macro F1 was `0.966`; per-label F1 was `0.957` for `analysis`, `0.941` for `hot_take`, and `1.000` for `reaction`. The held-out confusion matrix had one `hot_take` classified as `analysis`.

**Run note:** The required unit 5 training run completed locally and its metrics are committed. - *What I asked for:* Help stress-test the boundary between a supported analysis and an unsupported opinion.

- *What I asked for:* Check whether the acceptance criteria could be verified from held-out metrics.
